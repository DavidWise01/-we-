# WAG-239 · Resource Acquisition and Adaptive Demand

.dlw · {24/[2]/[4]/(3)/(3)/(1)/(1)}

WAG-239 tests the explicit new assumption that a finite external energy cache of 0.45 units is physically available to the modeled system. Each withdrawal is at most 0.012 units, with a three-tick delivery delay, 16% loss, and 0.001 energy activation fee. The cache does not replenish. Delayed arrivals can spill against the main energy capacity of 1. No environment-dependent free energy is inserted. The model also tests demand reduction: 28% discount of the actuator running cost with a 13% loss of effective shield intensity; this is an engineered tradeoff, not a general physical law. Alternatives are hybrid (both), acquire only, demand only, passive cache access but no use, and WAG-238 without the cache. The frozen WAG-235 decision table remains unchanged.

The controller observes past realized energy and modeled measured actuator expenditure; no future disturbance or scenario label enters its choice. The classifier is heuristic, not proof of indefinite resource availability. Note that all policies share background resource-generation physics, but **the acquisition mechanism is newly added only in the acquiring arms**. Improvements in those arms must not be presented as controller-only efficiency. Demand-only and passive are essential controls.
